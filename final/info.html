Available Software for PC

Software Essentials: Building your Digital Toolkit

Before diving into specific hobbies or work tasks, every computer needs a solid foundation. These are the "utilities" that keep your system running smoothly and safely.

A web browser is your gateway to the internet, and while Windows comes with Edge, many prefer Google Chrome for its speed or Mozilla Firefox for privacy. For security, Windows Security (built-in) is excellent, but adding Malwarebytes (Free version) is great for occasional deep scans to catch "adware" or "pup" files. For file management, 7-Zip is a tiny, free tool that lets you open ".zip" or ".rar" files—essential for downloading almost anything online. And when your hard drive gets full, TreeSize Free helps you see exactly what folders are taking up space.

The "Must-Haves" for Every PC

Regardless of what you do, start here:

  • Web Browser: While Windows comes with Edge, many prefer Google Chrome for its speed or Mozilla Firefox for privacy, and Opera GX for gamers.
  • Security: Windows Security (built-in) is excellent, but adding Malwarebytes (Free version) is great for occasional deep scans to catch "adware" or "pup" files.
  • File Management: 7-Zip is a tiny, free tool that lets you open ".zip" or ".rar" files—essential for downloading almost anything online.
  • Maintenance: TreeSize Free helps you see exactly what folders are taking up space when your hard drive gets full.

The Gamer’s Suite

If you bought your PC to play, you need "launchers" (digital storefronts) and communication tools.

  • Steam: The "Amazon" of video games. If a game exists, it’s probably on Steam.
  • Discord: The gold standard for talking to friends via text or voice while you play.
  • Epic Games Store: They give away a free game every single week—don't miss out!
  • GeForce Experience (Nvidia) or AMD Software: These keep your "Graphics Drivers" updated so your games don't crash and look their best.

The Content Creator’s Studio

For those looking to edit videos, design graphics, or start a YouTube channel without spending a fortune.

  • DaVinci Resolve: A professional-grade video editor used in Hollywood, but the basic version is completely free.
  • GIMP or Photopea: Excellent free alternatives to Adobe Photoshop for editing photos and making thumbnails.
  • OBS Studio: The industry standard for recording your screen or streaming live to Twitch and YouTube.
  • Audacity: A simple, powerful tool for recording and "cleaning up" your voice if you're making a podcast or voiceover.

The Office & Productivity Hub

Focusing on getting work done, staying organized, and communicating with a team.

  • Google Workspace or Microsoft 365: Whether you prefer Google Docs/Sheets or Word/Excel, you’ll need a "suite" for documents.
  • Slack or Microsoft Teams: The digital "office" where coworkers chat and share files.
  • Zoom: Still the most reliable way to jump into a video meeting with one click.
  • Adobe Acrobat Reader: Necessary for viewing and signing PDF contracts or forms.

The Student’s Backpack

Tools to help you research, write papers, and keep your schedule on track.

  • Notion: A "second brain." It’s a mix of a notebook, a to-do list, and a calendar—perfect for keeping track of different classes.
  • Grammarly: A digital writing assistant that catches typos and tone issues that standard spell-check misses.
  • Zotero: A lifesaver for research papers; it automatically formats your "Works Cited" and "Bibliography" pages for you.
  • Quizlet: Great for creating digital flashcards to study for exams on your PC or phone.

Pro Tip for Beginners

When installing new software, always choose the "Custom" installation option if available. This allows you to uncheck any "optional" add-ons that may come bundled with the software, such as toolbars or antivirus trials, which can slow down your computer or clutter your system with unwanted programs.

  • Be cautious when downloading software. Always download from the official website to avoid "bloatware" or "malware" that can come bundled with free downloads from sketchy sites.
  • Always download software directly from the official website. Avoid "third-party" download sites that look like they have everything in one place, as they often try to sneak extra, unwanted programs onto your computer!
  • Keep your software updated. Regular updates not only add new features but also patch security vulnerabilities that could be exploited by hackers.
  • Use a password manager. If you have accounts on multiple sites, a password manager like Bitwarden can generate and store strong, unique passwords for each one, keeping your online life secure.
